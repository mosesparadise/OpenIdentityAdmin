## Three distinct decisions

| Decision | Meaning | Does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current permissions | Current persisted, validated grants from enabled, eligible account state and exact approved bundle membership | Authority from token roles, user claims, cached grants or a role name alone |
| Usable platform administrator | An account currently configured to authenticate with the required factors and exercise the complete platform bundle | A currently logged-in session, actual human possession of credentials or future availability |
| Authorized actor | A currently valid authoritative full account session, current required permissions and fresh single-use authorization for the exact sensitive command | Authority from being counted as a usable survivor, recovery interactions or untrusted cookie timestamps |

Keep these policies cohesive and separate. `CeremonyAccount.IsActive` is only one input; it omits lockout, permissions and protected-factor availability. `JourneySessionState.Allows` is an actor-session input; applying it to the administrator population would incorrectly let logout/idle expiry remove a survivor. No boolean `isAdmin` substitutes for the decisions above.

## Current permission contract

The existing `AdministratorPermissions` catalogue defines four reserved bundle identities, ordinal permission type `identityplatform.permission`, and 25 distinct PlatformAdministrator permissions at version 1. It is the finite validation boundary, not a source of grants absent from storage.

1. Resolve current native user, role membership, role identity and role permission claims from the authoritative store. Recheck identifiers and normalization using exact ordinal comparisons after database retrieval, including on SQL Server. Reject case/padding aliases, duplicate/conflicting relevant records and malformed reserved authority; database collation equality alone is insufficient.
2. Grant only currently persisted permission values that belong to that exact role's approved finite bundle. Missing known claims are removals and remain removed. Union valid grants across current recognized memberships; do not recreate a complete bundle from its name. Wildcards, unknown values or out-of-bundle permission claims deny the evaluation rather than being silently adopted.
3. User claims, token/cookie permission claims and unrecognized custom roles grant no authority. A reserved permission claim attached to an unrecognized role is malformed authority and denies. Unrelated nonpermission claims may exist but confer no grant. Duplicate persisted membership keys, duplicate permission claims within the same role (same role/type/value), or ambiguous normalized reserved role identities deny rather than being repaired. The same valid permission contributed by two different recognized roles is legitimate set union, not a duplicate-record fault.
4. Unknown requested permissions deny. Missing user/state, disabled/suspended/ineligible state, unavailable database/protection or unsafe clock state deny. Return typed safe outcomes; no raw provider exceptions, seeds or submitted values enter diagnostics.
5. Do not cache this decision across requests or nodes. Any in-request view is immutable and owned by its current operation; mutations reread inside serialization. M02-D must later prove actual API/BFF revocation latency, bearer/session binding and direct-HTTP denial. This internal contract does not accept those endpoints.

Reserved bundle identities and the code-owned maximum manifest are immutable in this increment. Rename/delete of a reserved role or adding a value beyond its finite bundle is unsupported and denied even to a platform administrator. Removing/restoring a permitted persisted grant is a governed authority change. Because PlatformAdministrator is a shared role and every usable survivor requires its complete 25 grants, removing one of those grants affects every member and is denied by the voluntary survivor guard regardless of member count. Later custom-role/resource APIs remain M05; they must use these boundaries rather than introduce a generic bypass.

## Exact usable-platform-administrator predicate

At an accepted authoritative UTC sample, all of the following must hold for the same account, with exact IDs, coherent current rows and protected material:

- The native user exists, is enabled, has `TwoFactorEnabled == true`, a configured native password, a nonempty security stamp and lockout enabled. Password presence uses the native manager; no credential is requested, hashed anew or exposed while counting survivors.
- Enrollment is `Active`, not suspended, has a positive factor generation and valid nonnegative revision. Pending, Consumed, RecoveryRequired and unknown state never count. Authentication state exists with a positive generation and internally valid budgets/revisions.
- Native and product lockout checks pass the current `CeremonyBudget.IsAvailable` semantics, including malformed-count/lockout rejection. The durable clock fence is healthy. Counting does not clear failures, unlock an account, advance factor generations or manufacture an authentication proof.
- The active factor belongs to this exact account/generation and is readable through the current protected native store using the configured protection ring. Missing/corrupt/transplanted material or missing required keys cannot count. The lookup must explicitly target active material, without inheriting `FactorAccess.Staged`; the current protected store prioritizes staged access, so a generic native key read in that ambient scope is insufficient. A valid staged replacement cannot hide a missing/corrupt active factor. `TwoFactorEnabled` or a factor row alone is insufficient.
- Current validated membership includes the exact reserved `PlatformAdministrator` role, and the grants persisted on that PlatformAdministrator role itself contain **all 25** permissions of the complete v1 platform bundle. A missing grant means that account no longer counts; overlapping permissions from another role cannot fill that gap. The same union through other roles, user claims or a custom role cannot substitute for this reserved platform membership.

A live session, recent login, spare recovery code, browser interaction and observed human possession are not required to count configured primary authentication capability. They also cannot replace any missing requirement. This deliberately conservative predicate protects voluntary changes at their transaction boundary; it cannot guarantee that a person retained their password/authenticator or that no later security event will make the account unusable.

For an authorization decision, enforce the additional authoritative session bindings already embodied by `JourneySessionProof`/`JourneySessionState`: account ID, session ID, generation, factor generation, security stamp, truthful `pwd otp` methods, idle/absolute limits and safe time. Restricted enrollment/recovery capabilities never satisfy it.

## Mutation and sensitive-proof contract

T020 supplies internal policy and manager-backed orchestration within the existing Core/Infrastructure `Features/Identity` boundary. No public management route, CLI grant/reset switch or alternate host is introduced by this design. Existing thin Programs, Composition extensions and explicit Features/Setup registration remain. T021 uses the existing dedicated Administrator Aspire test project; story/count changes must be reviewed before implementation, without extending the 660-second invocation ceiling.

Every supported command has one fixed action identity, exact actor/session, canonical target identifiers, allowlisted requested changes, and expected versions. Treat the request as untrusted. Derive the affected identities from current stored relationships, not a caller-provided survivor list. The internal orchestration must reject unsupported operations rather than dispatch arbitrary manager methods. Initial governance commands require existing coherent administrator state for every required account lock; they do not silently create authentication/enrollment rows for nominees.

| Intended policy boundary | Required authority before mutation | Additional requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assign/remove a reserved bundle | `users.write` and `administrators.assign` | Full current actor authorization; target enrollment is never fabricated |
| Change persisted reserved bundle grants | `roles.permissions` and `administrators.assign` | Values stay within that bundle's finite catalogue; evaluate every affected platform survivor |
| Disable/enable or suspend/resume a privileged account | `users.disable` and `administrators.assign` | Pending/recovery/corrupt state cannot be converted to Active by toggling flags |
| Delete a privileged account or any future indirect destructive operation | `users.write` and `administrators.assign` for user deletion; future distinct actions need their own explicit policy | Same survivor guard; deletion endpoint/lifecycle remains M05 |
| Rename/delete a reserved role or grant unknown/out-of-bundle permission | None can authorize it in v1 | Deny as unsupported; no override flag |

Ordinary role editing, user administration or future bulk operations must not indirectly grant/revoke privileged authority without `administrators.assign`. Include role memberships, persisted claim edits, role identity changes, user deletion and account/factor usability transitions in the analysis. The table defines shared policies, not a claim that every listed management operation is implemented in T020.

Sensitive authorization follows the already approved self-service security pattern: current full session plus freshly verified password and unused native TOTP. The proof is single-use and bound to actor, initiating session, exact action, target IDs, canonical requested changes and expected versions. Freshness is strictly less than the existing 300-second ceiling; future timestamps deny. Password success alone never clears factor failures. Verification shares the existing bounded native replay ledger and failure/lockout policy.

Use private-factory, transaction-owned proof capabilities; do not accept an externally constructed proof, method string, `amr`/`auth_time` claim or arbitrary recent-cookie flag. Existing `CeremonyProof` is bound to a supported interaction and cannot simply be relabeled for a governance action. T020 must explicitly integrate action binding, expiry and consumption with the existing mechanism, and supply negative tests before any caller is accepted. No new HTTP step-up journey is implied here. Changed payload/versions require new authorization; failed or ambiguous operations are not automatically retried.

Authorize against current **pre-mutation** state immediately before protected writes while holding the relevant locks. A legitimate self-demotion may then remove the actor's own authority if another usable administrator remains. Post-mutation checks validate the intended survivor invariant, not a requirement that a self-demoted actor retain the permission just used.

## Concurrency and atomicity

The existing governance `Revision` is incremented to acquire its portable UPDATE lock, including during enrollment/recovery. It is not a stable authority-policy version. Authentication revision also advances for unrelated ceremonies/activity. Neither should be exposed as a purportedly stable role-edit version.

T020 will add a nonnegative portable `AuthorityRevision` to the **existing** governance singleton, initialized additively to zero without altering `BootstrapConsumed`, receipts or existing grants. It changes once per committed governed authority command (membership, permitted bundle-claim changes and governed privileged account-state changes), never for reads, rejected operations, rolled-back writes, ordinary session activity or lock acquisition alone. Overflow fails closed. Both-provider migrations and schema readiness must precede use; no database-specific rowversion dependency is introduced.

Commands bind that authority revision plus native target user/role concurrency stamps and the enrollment revision for product-state intent, using an explicit composite version where several targets are affected. Recheck the original expected values after acquisition; check every native manager `IdentityResult`. Ordinary lockout, recovery and session/factor changes may not update AuthorityRevision, so current account locks/state/generations/proof remain independently mandatory. The version prevents stale intent; it does not replace serialization.

The required mutation sequence is:

1. Discover the complete candidate lock plan and affected target/witness IDs without treating that discovery as authoritative. Snapshot and sort it immutably. A preselected survivor witness may be used to bound work; an omitted candidate can cause conservative denial, never acceptance through a weaker predicate.
2. Open the existing bounded operation transaction; acquire governance, admission if actually needed, sorted browser rows if needed, sorted authentication-account rows, then dependent rows in the established order. Include actor, targets and every survivor witness whose usability is relied upon. No late acquisition of an earlier-sorting account or earlier lock class is allowed.
3. Reread native/product records and all relevant memberships/claims after acquisition. Validate the complete affected set and witness relationships. A changed set requiring undeclared locks fails as stale; do not expand the plan mid-transaction or silently retry.
4. Recheck expected versions, current actor/session/permissions, clock/budgets and command-bound proof. Evaluate the projected state and require at least one fully usable platform administrator after an ordinary voluntary destructive change. A count read before locking is not authority.
5. Apply supported native Identity changes through managers and product changes in the same scoped DbContext/transaction. Recheck time/proof before the protected boundary; consume the one-time capability/replay reservation and increment the authority version with the successful change. A real provider failure, cancellation, stale stamp or exhausted deadline denies and rolls back.
6. Append exactly one accepted governance audit intent for the committed transition in the same transaction, with separate actual actor and target identities, fixed action/outcome, accepted time and a nonsecret correlation ID. Existing `CeremonyAudit` assumes actor=target and cannot be reused unchanged for cross-account governance. A purpose-specific writer must retain atomicity. Do not serialize payloads/credentials or hide metadata in a free-form event string.
7. Commit before returning success. Independent verification compares pre/post native and product versions/state with that audit. Denial auditing must not commit partial target changes; an audit-write failure cannot produce a successful mutation. Lost output means unknown completion until current state and durable audit are reconciled; do not replay a sensitive command to discover whether it committed.

The five-second existing operation ceiling covers lock contention and work; no blanket retries or enlarged timeout are approved. All concurrent governance mutations share the singleton. Ordinary password failures can lock out an account without it, so the chosen survivor's authentication lock must remain held until commit. A later mandatory security event may still legitimately leave zero usable administrators. The guarantee is the serialized decision at commit, not permanent availability.

Direct database modification by a privileged operator is outside this application guarantee and is never a supported recovery mechanism. Managers alone do not enforce the product policy; every future application writer of relevant state must enter the governed boundary or the explicitly distinct security path below. Architecture and behavioral tests must prevent alternate manager/EF write paths from becoming exposed management operations.

## Recovery and mandatory security enforcement

| Situation | Supported behavior | Last-administrator treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sole administrator voluntarily removes membership/grants, disables/suspends self or destroys the usable factor | Deny unless another locked, currently usable platform administrator survives | A recovery code, pending nominee, disabled account or restricted interaction is not a substitute witness |
| Sole administrator uses existing password + unused recovery code | Consume the code once, invalidate prior authority and enter restricted replacement; require verified new factor before full authentication | Allowed existing recovery transition even though RecoveryRequired does not count as usable; it grants no temporary administration |
| Voluntary factor replacement/regeneration with fresh proof | Preserve the existing staged/atomic behavior; old active factor remains until valid replacement commits where specified | Do not turn staging into a flag-based last-admin exemption |
| Native/product lockout, detected invalid factor/protection, or authorized compromise containment | Deny/revoke unsafe authority even if the usable population reaches zero | Never suppress lockout, restore a revoked session, relax MFA or falsify a survivor count |
| Temporary lockout expires with intact primary credentials | Existing login may succeed only after all current checks and real factor proof pass | No automatic session/role restoration or administrator-count override |
| Disabled/suspended account, lost password, all factors/codes lost, or final recovery interaction lost with no remaining valid proof | Fail closed; retain the incident and seek a separately designed authorized recovery process | No current emergency-reset route, bootstrap reopening, direct SQL recipe or implicit operator bypass |

Mandatory security enforcement is a separate fixed application path, not `force=true`, a caller-selected reason or a privileged override of the ordinary command. Existing lockout/recovery paths remain authoritative. A new operator compromise-containment or all-factor-loss workflow would need its own threat model, authenticated authority and explicit approval; this design does not create one. `users.recovery` and `users.unlock` strings alone do not implement such workflows.

Initial enrollment currently requires the permanent bootstrap receipt owner. Assigning a reserved role to a second account **does not implement second-administrator onboarding**. An unenrolled nominee never counts as a survivor. M05 account/factor lifecycle must supply a reviewed second-administrator onboarding route before claiming a delivered handoff. T020/T021 may use valid additional-administrator fixtures to exercise policy, clearly labeled as fixtures; flags alone cannot stand in for protected factor/native state. This limitation belongs in operational documentation and remains a product dependency.
